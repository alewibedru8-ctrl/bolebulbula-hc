Trend interpretation

  • Direction: a clear upward trajectory, from 15.0% in new week 1 to 65.1% in new week 14, a gain of 50.1 percentage points.
  • Slope: a simple linear trend across the 14 observations is about +3.4 percentage points per week. The direction is real, though weekly values are not linear.
  • Two phases: new weeks 1 to 10 were highly variable (15.0–51.3%); new weeks 11 to 14 formed a higher phase at 69.4%, 66.7%, 60.3% and 65.1%.
  • Early versus late: mean concordance was 30.5% in new weeks 1 to 4 and 65.4% in new weeks 11 to 14, an improvement of 34.9 points.
  • Stability: variability is still substantial (standard deviation about 16.7 points), so this is emerging stabilization, not full process control.

Service-level priorities

OPD-103 is the highest-priority department to investigate, followed by OPD-208. Differences in OPD-6 are comparatively small. The program should focus on recurring, operationally meaningful discrepancies and not treat every numerical difference as a failure.

Family planning is read with the documented contextual rule: an E-Register excess can be an accepted operational match when it comes from long-acting FP clients who are not represented in the EMR count. Document such cases; do not automatically classify them as data-quality failures.

Closed-loop QI actions

  • Keep the weekly EMR and E-Register triangulation, using one standard comparison template.
  • Classify every discrepancy by root cause: missed entry, late entry, duplicate entry, wrong service or date, transcription or source issue, or a valid service-specific difference.
  • Trace the source-to-register pathway for OPD-103 and OPD-208 first, and correct what is found.
  • Check disputed entries against the original source sheet or image, and document corrections or explanations within 24 to 48 hours where required.
  • Plot the re-baselined run chart each week and review both the level and the stability of performance.
  • Aim first to sustain 70% or more, then 80%, then 90% or more as high reliability.
  • Once high concordance holds, move from intensive review to risk-based sampling, keeping periodic full triangulation.

Conclusion

After re-baselining at original week 6, EMR and E-Register concordance rose clearly over the next 14 weeks, from 15.0% to 65.1%. The last four weeks held a much higher level (60.3–69.4%) than the early weeks, which indicates real improvement and emerging stabilization. Concordance has not yet reached the 70% milestone and week-to-week variability remains, so the right management position is improving, but not yet reliably controlled.

Data and interpretation notes
  • The re-baselined series has 14 weekly observations, 574 comparable checks and 279 exact matches.
  • Pooled strict exact-match concordance for the period is 48.6%. This is different from the final-week value of 65.1%.
  • Strict numerical concordance should not be read mechanically where documented service-context differences exist.
  • No revised concordance percentage is given after contextual family planning reclassification, because complete row-level reclassification is not available in the source report.

High

The standard calls for a fence that keeps out pets and other animals, protects vulnerable patients and has at least two gates that support emergency movement. It was marked not met.

  • Uncontrolled access, animal entry and reduced security.
  • Emergency access may be inadequate, which can delay evacuation or movement.
  • Vulnerable clients face greater exposure to environmental and security hazards.

Corrective action

  • Assess the perimeter and record weak, missing or damaged sections.
  • Draw up a costed plan for a secure fence and suitable gates.
  • Design for routine access control and emergency evacuation.
  • Start with the highest-risk sections while mobilizing authorized resources.
  • Verify completion by joint management and quality inspection.

Standard 16 · Adequate, continuous water supply

Critical

The standard requires water every hour of every day, in enough quantity for service areas, with reliable drinking water for staff, patients and caregivers. It was marked not met.

  • Service continuity and infection prevention are at risk.
  • Hand hygiene, cleaning and sanitation can be compromised when water is not assured.
  • An interruption can affect several departments at once.

Corrective action

  • Assess daily demand, supply capacity, storage capacity and how often supply is interrupted.
  • Write a water-continuity plan covering normal, stored and emergency supply.
  • Provide adequate storage and keep reservoirs hygienic.
  • Monitor water availability at critical service points.
  • Coordinate with the responsible authorities and leadership for a sustainable supply.
